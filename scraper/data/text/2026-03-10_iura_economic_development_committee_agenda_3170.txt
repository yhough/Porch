A. Review of the Density District Priority Business Loan Fund Program — Discussion
Bohn explained that the meeting agenda packet includes a memorandum concerning the Committee’s
review of the Downtown Density District Priority Business Loan Fund (PB-LF).
Key Discussion Points
1. Loan Program Structure & Purpose
The IURA currently manages several small loan programs, originally created for different purposes over the
past decades. Only two programs are actively used today:
• Citywide CD Revolving Loan Fund (gap financing, capped at $100–150k, prohibits >35% alcohol sales)

EDC Meeting Minutes
February 10, 2026
Page 2 of 3
• Priority Business Loan Fund (more flexible, supports alcohol related businesses, targeted to the
downtown density district)
‑
Members questioned if so many separate loan programs are in fact needed, given the small available loan
balance (~$250k).
2. Outdated 2007 Priority Business List
The current list of priority business types (from 2007) is widely considered outdated and overly narrow.
Committee members agreed that:
• The list should be replaced with broad categories and program goals, not specific business types.
• The loan program should encourage flexibility rather than restrict applicants.
3. Incorporating Retail Study Findings
A recent City funded retail study highlighted promising sectors like:
• Arts & entertainment
‑
• Destination food & beverage
• Vintage/consignment retail
• Diversified entertainment
Members agreed these areas may be more realistic and impactful than trying to attract “essential
services” like pharmacies or groceries. They also recognized challenges with locating a downtown grocery,
particularly due to delivery logistics and competition from Wegmans and Aldi.
4. Alcohol & Cannabis Eligibility
The committee discussed loosening or removing the CD Fund’s 35% alcohol sales limit, which prevents
aiding many restaurants and bars. Cannabis businesses may also need future policy consideration as State
‑
rules evolve.
5. Equity & Inclusion
Support for underrepresented business owners should remain a priority, regardless of the specific business
type.
6. Loan Fund Constraints
HUD’s $35,000-per-job funding requirement still applies. Loan underwriting and risk management remain
concerns, but Committee members expressed a preference for flexible, case by case review, rather than
rigid prohibitions.
‑ ‑
7. Next Steps
Staff will:
• Draft a revised loan program framework with broad eligibility categories.
• Consider alcohol and cannabis policy updates.
• Share drafts with the Downtown Ithaca Alliance for feedback.

PROJECT DESCRIPTION
In the space below, provide a clear project summary that includes a description of the proposed project. Include the
Census tract number in which the project will be located (see Application Instructions). If your project includes job
placement, list your employer partners and describe the commitments they have made to the project.
The Hospitality Employment Training Program (HETP) provides barrier-informed workforce training to unemployed
and underemployed adults (18+) in Tompkins County, with a focus on City of Ithaca residents. Through staff
expertise and community partnerships, HETP prepares participants for employment in hospitality, customer service,
childcare, and office administration. In this program year, HETP will train 20 job seekers and place at least 14 into
employment. Since 2014, HETP has delivered a comprehensive training model that combines classroom instruction,
hands-on experience, and wraparound supports. Participants build job readiness skills, gain industry exposure, and
access supportive services that promote long-term self-sufficiency and well-being. The program functions as a bridge
between job seekers and employers by pairing classroom training with structured internships supported by formal
worksite agreements, ensuring accountability for both participants and employers while responding directly to local
workforce needs.
HETP has served 235 adults since its inception, including both walk-in clients and full program participants. Of those
served, 25% are ages 18-24, 45% identify as Black/African American, and 28% identify as Asian, Latino, or
multi-ethnic-populations that face persistent employment barriers and are often under served by traditional
workforce programs. More recently, participants with significant language barriers have utilized HETP alongside
English as a Second Language classes to strengthen their English skills, build cultural familiarity, and expand
professional networks in support of employment goals.
A core component of HETP is addressing the barriers that interfere with employment stability, particularly childcare,
transportation, and overall well-being. Program staff have developed new classroom curriculum focused on
self-management and well-being, including wellness, emotional regulation, motivation, organization, time
management, and decision-making. Professional partners such as Tompkins Whole Health and the Mental Health
Association support this work by providing expertise when participants require additional guidance.
Childcare access remains one of the most significant barriers to employment, and HETP is intentionally expanding its
impact in this area in 2026. Staff assist participants with securing and funding childcare, navigating applications, and
advocating for consistent care, including providing on-site childcare support when needed. Through partnerships with
the Child Development Council, Ithaca Community Childcare Center, and Downtown Ithaca Children's Center, HETP
also aims to place participants into childcare employment- either with established providers or by supporting
individuals interested in opening in-home childcare programs.
Transportation is another critical focus area. HETP supports participants through bus passes, access to GIAC
vehicles, refurbished bicycles, driver education, and instruction on local transportation options. Representatives from
TCAT, the Human Services Coalition (211 ), and the Tompkins County Department of Transportation meet directly
with cohorts to provide transportation education, register participants for GO Ithaca, and offer one-on-one coaching
to ensure participants can effectively navigate available resources.
As workforce shortages continue across hospitality, childcare, and service sectors-particularly for second-and
third-shift positions-HETP addresses an urgent and growing need for skilled, supported workers in Tompkins
County. With more than 11 years of demonstrated success, the program delivers measurable outcomes that extend
beyond job placement, including improved job retention, increased workplace readiness, and pathways to stable
wages and career advancement. Participants-many of whom are parents, young adults, immigrants, and individuals
from historically marginalized communities- benefit from a program intentionally designed to reduce inequities
through language access, barrier removal, and individualized support. HETP qualifies as a work requirement to
maintain social service benefits such as SNAP, Cash Assistance, Housing and more.
Program activities are primarily based at GIAC in Census Tract 8 and are supported by a strong network of
community agencies, presenters, and employer partners. Continued investment in HETP will allow the program to
expand training capacity, deepen childcare and transportation supports, and strengthen employer partnerships
ensuring both participants and local businesses are positioned for long-term, sustainable success.

POPULATION SERVED & PROJECT IMPACT
If your project will provide job training and placement, describe the population the project will serve, being sure to
include income levels (i.e., 30% AMI, 50% AMI, 80% AMI), and any special needs. How has the project been designed
to address the specific needs of this population?
The Hospitality Employment Training Program (HETP) serves low-and moderate-income adults age 18 and older
residing in Tompkins County who meet CDBG income eligibility guidelines. More than 80% of participants are below
80% of Area Median Income (AMI), with many falling at or below 50%.
The population served includes individuals with disabilities, formerly incarcerated individuals, adults experiencing
homelessness or housing instability, individuals in substance use recovery, those with mental health challenges, and
participants facing language barriers or chronic employment instability.
HETP is intentionally designed to address these layered challenges through individualized, barrier-informed services.
Each participant meets one-on-one with staff to assess needs and develop a personalized employment action plan.
Weekly case management sessions ensure progress is monitored and barriers are addressed in real time. The program
provides coordinated referrals for housing support, childcare assistance, transportation resources, healthcare, and other
essential services to stabilize participants so they can fully engage in training.
Targeted interventions are tailored to specific needs. Young adults receive workplace readiness and mentoring support;
individuals reentering society receive assistance navigating legal and employment restrictions; and participants
managing disabilities, mental health conditions, or substance use disorders are supported through accommodations,
healthcare referrals, and coordination with service providers. Through this holistic, person-centered model, HETP builds
pathways toward long-term economic stability.
Explain the project goal(s). How will each goal be measured and documented to confirm whether or not it has been
met?
The primary goal of the Hospitality Employment Training Program (HETP) is to prepare unemployed and
underemployed adults in Tompkins County for sustainable employment in hospitality, childcare, customer service, and
office administration. For this funding cycle, HETP will train 20 participants and place at least 14 into employment. This
goal will be measured through participant completion of classroom instruction, required workshops, and internship
hours. Progress is tracked using the HETP Program Completion Matrix, which documents attendance, participation,
soft-skill development, internship benchmarks, and movement from intake to completion status. Weekly one-on-one
case management meetings further document individual progress and readiness. Completion is confirmed when
participants meet required training hours, demonstrate workplace competencies, and fulfill their individualized action
plan goals.
HETP addresses barriers related to childcare, transportation, housing instability, and overall well-being. Progress toward
barrier reduction is documented through case management notes, completed referrals, transportation supports issued,
childcare arrangements secured, and engagement with partner agencies. Advancement within the Program Completion
Matrix also reflects increased stability and employment readiness.
Employment outcomes are measured and documented through employer verification, offer letters, or pay stubs.
Internship host evaluations and executed worksite agreements provide additional documentation of job readiness.
HETP conducts follow-up communication with both participants and employers to track short-term retention, confirm
continued employment, and address any emerging barriers.
Together, the Program Completion Matrix, Trainee Certification Chart, employer documentation, and case management
records provide quantifiable evidence of training completion, barrier reduction, job placement, and retention-confirming
whether project goals have been successfully met.

POPULATION SERVED & PROJECT IMPACT (cont.)
Will your project advance the City's goal of ending and preventing homelessness? How?
Yes - HETP will advance the City's goal of ending and preventing homelessness by addressing key drivers of housing
instability through workforce readiness, barrier reduction, and coordinated support. In Tompkins County, housing
affordability challenges continue to worsen: over half of renters are cost-burdened (spending more than 30% of income
on housing) and nearly 38% are severely cost-burdened (more than half of income), with rental and home prices rising
substantially in recent years. These conditions contribute to longer episodes of homelessness and make exiting
homelessness more difficult.
HETP targets adults facing employment barriers - including those experiencing housing instability - and connects
employment outcomes to greater housing stability and homelessness prevention. Sustainable employment is widely
recognized as one of the strongest predictors of housing retention; increased income allows participants to afford rising
rents and reduces the risk of falling into homelessness. HETP's weekly case management helps participants address
barriers like transportation, childcare, and housing needs in real time, connecting them to supportive services and
referrals that stabilize their circumstances early. Financial education is offered in one on one sessions and as a training
with Alternatives Credit Union.
By training participants for livable-wage jobs, placing them in employment, and coordinating wraparound supports (e.g.,
childcare navigation, housing referrals, benefit access, financial literacy), HETP directly increases participants' economic
stability - a core factor in preventing homelessness. As the local homeless response system reports long shelter stays
and low exits into permanent housing partly due to scarce affordable housing and high costs, HETP's model mitigates
this by equipping individuals with the income and supports needed to secure and maintain housing rather than cycling
through emergency systems. Through its person-centered, employment-focused approach, HETP contributes to
reducing both the incidence and duration of homelessness in the community.
Will your project advance the City's goal of moving people out of poverty? How?
Yes - HETP advances the City's goal of moving people out of poverty by addressing both economic and personal
barriers to sustainable employment. Many participants face underemployment alongside challenges such as mental
health instability, inconsistent routines, limited work history, childcare needs, transportation barriers, and housing
instability.
HETP provides hands-on job readiness training, soft skill development, industry exposure, and individualized case
management that help participants build consistent daily routines, strengthen confidence, and develop the coping,
communication, and workplace skills necessary for a healthy, sustainable work life. Milestone-based stipends tied to
program completion provide immediate financial support to cover essentials such as transportation and childcare while
reinforcing accountability, engagement, and consistency.
By connecting participants to livable-wage employment, supporting job retention, and providing wraparound services
that stabilize housing, childcare, and health needs, HETP increases income and economic self-sufficiency. Through this
holistic approach, the program equips participants to move out of poverty and achieve long-term stability for themselves
and their families.

PROMOTION OF FAIR HOUSING
How will your project address any of the factors contributing ("Contributing Factors") to fair housing issues and
problems in the City of Ithaca? Refer to: Explanation of IURA Assessment of Fair Housing Contributing Factors
document.
HETP addresses several contributing factors to fair housing challenges in the City of Ithaca by strengthening
economic stability, increasing housing literacy, and reducing barriers that disproportionately impact low-income
households and marginalized communities.
Many HETP participants enter the program with low or no income and face housing instability driven by cost burden,
limited affordable inventory, discrimination, and difficulty navigating complex systems. By providing barrier-informed
workforce training, job placement support, stipends tied to program milestones, and individualized case
management, HETP increases income stability and strengthens participants' ability to secure and maintain housing.
Consistent employment and predictable earnings are among the strongest protections against eviction and housing
displacement.
Beyond employment, HETP supports participants in navigating public benefits, childcare access, transportation
systems, and housing resources. The program connects participants to agencies that protect tenant rights and
address discriminatory practices, helping them understand source-of-income protections and how to advocate for
themselves. Partnerships with organizations such as the Mental Health Association expand access to wellbeing
supports, recognizing that mental health instability can directly affect housing retention and the ability to navigate
landlord relationships.
HETP's curriculum further develops communication, conflict resolution, financial literacy, and cultural competency
skills. These competencies strengthen participants' ability to engage in lease negotiations, manage household
finances, resolve disputes, and recognize discriminatory treatment. By combining economic mobility strategies with
practical housing navigation skills and community referrals, HETP works to reduce systemic barriers that contribute
to fair housing inequities and promotes long-term housing stability for adults facing employment and income
disparities in Ithaca and Tompkins County.

services to adults facing barriers such as housing instability, mental health challenges, limited education, and economic

As a department of the City of Ithaca, GIAC operates within established municipal systems for fiscal oversight,

Program Coordinator Lonnie Jenkins brings both professional and lived experience to the role, with a background in

Describe your staffing plan for the proposed project. Indicate what percentage of each staff member's time will be

employment.

be impacted, potentially limiting job placement opportunities.
While GIAC Administration provides fiscal oversight and operational support, sustained staffing at current levels is
essential to maintaining the program's intensity and holistic service model. Reduced funding would directly affect
participant support, program completion rates, and overall employment outcomes.

FUNDRAISING INITIATIVES
Regarding the secured vs. unsecured funding section of your Excel budget application form, please describe in detail
below what fundraising initiatives your organization has undertaken over the past year and/or plans to undertake in
the year ahead to identify/secure additional funding and ensure the financial viability of your project/program (even if
you have pursued funding that was declined by the time of your IURA application). The IURA is interested in knowing
what your organization does or will be doing on an ongoing basis to support the program/project.
HETP maintains financial viability through a combination of secured funding, in-kind contributions, employer
partnerships, and proactive grant seeking. The program is supported by the Greater Ithaca Activities Center (GIAC),
a department of the City of Ithaca, which provides ongoing operational funding for staffing, facilities, and participant
services. HETP also receives competitive United Way funding on a biennial cycle, currently supporting a portion of
childcare and special event costs. In addition, HETP consistently applies for local funding opportunities, including the
Strategic Tourism Planning Board's Tourism Implementation Grant.
In-kind partnerships significantly strengthen program delivery while reducing costs. Tompkins Workforce NY provides
resume and cover letter workshops, mock interviews, and access to subsidized training, allowing participants to
practice phone, in-person, and virtual interviewing skills. Community partners such as CDRC, Alternatives Credit
Union, and Mid-State COSH deliver workshops in conflict resolution, active listening, financial education, and
workplace safety. Tompkins Chamber, and Visit Ithaca provide and excellent guide to local tourism attractions as well
as networking opportunities for the program and its participants. Jennifer's Closet, and the Women's Opportunity
Center provide etiquette and professionalism training along with professional attire and work uniforms at no cost to
participants.
Employer partnerships further support program outcomes by offering on-the-job training placements and potential
permanent employment. While these temporary training opportunities represent strong commitments, outcomes
depend on participant readiness and employer availability. HETP does not seek reimbursement from nonprofit
internship sites; instead, internships are treated as workforce pipelines that provide hands-on experience,
professional networking, and realistic pathways to permanent employment.
Looking ahead, HETP continues expanding partnerships to meet emerging participant needs. Collaboration with the
Mental Health Association has strengthened the curriculum in wellbeing and provides a trusted referral resource for
participants experiencing mental health challenges. A partnership with a local community garden introduces food
growing and preparation skills, promoting financial sustainability amid rising grocery costs. By continuously identifying
new funding opportunities and responsive partnerships, HETP ensures its training model evolves alongside the
barriers affecting participants. This diversified approach sustains high-quality programming while serving adults
facing employment challenges in Tompkins County.
