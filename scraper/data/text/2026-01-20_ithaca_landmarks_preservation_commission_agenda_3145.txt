RESOLUTION: First Presbyterian Church, 315 North Cayuga Street, DeWitt Park Historic
District - Proposal to Construct a Barrier Free Access Ramp on the West Elevation
WHEREAS, First Presbyterian Church, 315 South Geneva Street, is located in the DeWitt Park
Historic District, as designated under Section 228-3 of the City of Ithaca Municipal
Code in 1971, and as listed on the New York State and National Registers of
Historic Places in 1971, and
WHEREAS, as set forth in Section 228-4 of the Municipal Code, an Application for a Certificate
of Appropriateness dated November 25, 2025, was submitted for review to the
Ithaca Landmarks Preservation Commission (ILPC) by property owner First
Presbyterian Church, including the following: (1) two narratives respectively titled
Description of Proposed Change(s) and Reasons for Changes(s); (2) a thirteen page
packet prepared by Whitham Planning, Design, Landscape Architecture, PLLC,
dated November 25, 2025, and including a “Building History;” “Existing
Conditions” site plan; “Ramp and Railings within Historic Districts – Precedents”
photographs; a narrative addressing “What we are proposing,” “Why we are
proposing,” and “How do the design and material resonate with the context;” a
proposed ramp design; and the minutes from the June 24, 1971 Ithaca Landmarks
Preservation Commission meeting, and
WHEREAS, the ILPC has also reviewed the New York State Building-Structure Inventory Form
for the First Presbyterian Church, 301 North Cayuga Street, and the City of Ithaca’s
DeWitt Park Historic District Summary Statement, and
WHEREAS, the proposed project involves the removal of red sandstone steps at the
northwest entrance of the Cayuga Street facade and the construction of a
concrete barrier free access ramp, landscape stairs, and walkways in that
location, and
WHEREAS, the issuance of a Certificate of Appropriateness is a Type II Action under the New
York State Environmental Quality Review Act and the City Environmental Quality
Review Ordinance for which no further environmental review is required, and
WHEREAS, the applicant (has/has not) provided sufficient documentation and information to
evaluate impacts of the proposal on the subject property and surrounding properties,
and
WHEREAS, a Public Hearing for the purpose of considering approval of the Application for
Certificate of Appropriateness was conducted at the regularly scheduled ILPC
meeting on January 20,2026, now therefore be it
RESOLVED, that the ILPC has made the following findings of fact concerning the property and
the proposal:
1

ILPC Meeting – January 20, 2026
As identified in the City of Ithaca’s DeWitt Park Historic District Summary
Statement, the period of significance for the area now known as the DeWitt Park
Historic District is 1820-1930.
As indicated in the New York State Building-Structure Inventory Form, the
Romanesque-Revival First Presbyterian Church at 315 North Cayuga Street was
designed by New York City architect J. Cleveland Cady and was constructed in 1900.
Constructed within the period of significance of the DeWitt Park Historic District
and possessing a high level of architectural integrity, the property is a contributing
element of the DeWitt Park Historic District.
Located the at northwest corner of DeWitt Park, the landscape and hardscape
around the First Presbyterian Church contribute to the historic quality of the Ithaca
first “public square”. These features are particularly important along the south and
west elevations of the church structure.
In consideration of this and all approvals of proposals for alterations, new
construction or demolition in historic districts, the ILPC must determine that
the proposed exterior work will not have a substantial adverse effect on the
aesthetic, historical or architectural significance and value of either the
landmark or, if the improvement is within a district, of the neighboring
improvements in such district. In considering architectural and cultural
value, the Commission shall consider whether the proposed change is
consistent with the historic value and the spirit of the architectural style of the
landmark or district in accordance with Section 228-6 of the Municipal Code.
In making this determination, the Commission is guided by the principles set
forth in Section 228-6B of the Municipal Code, as further elaborated in
Section 228-6C, and by the Secretary of the Interior’s Standards for
Rehabilitation, and in this case specifically the following principles and
Standards:
Principle #2 The historic features of a property located within, and
contributing to the significance of, an historic district shall be altered as little
as possible and any alterations made shall be compatible with both the
historic character of the individual property and the character of the district as
a whole.
Principle #3 New construction located within an historic district shall be
compatible with the historic character of the district within which it is
located.
Standard #2 The historic character of a property will be retained and
preserved. The removal of distinctive materials or alteration of features and
spaces that characterize a property will be avoided.
Standard #9 New additions, exterior alterations, or related new construction
shall not destroy historic materials that characterize the property. The new
work shall be differentiated from the old and shall be compatible with the

What are we proposing?
The First Presbyterian Church is proposing the installation of an accessible walkway on the west
(Cayuga Street) elevation of the building. A gentle,quarter-circlearc layout that reflects the
Romanesque Revival architectural language of the building,connectingto the walkway between the
park and the church.
Why are we proposing?
The primary purpose of this proposal is to provide universal access and equity of entrance to all who
visit the church. The current conditions lack an accessible route to the main interior space, limiting
access for individuals using wheelchairs, strollers, or with mobility impairments.
This intervention will not only fulfill ADA Compliance but also expressthe church’s core values of
inclusivity and hospitality. As noted in the church’s communication with the ILPC earlier in June
2024, this effort also addresses concerns for safe egress during power outages or emergencies,
complementing the church’s broader commitment to accessibility and safety.
The following outlines three specific past circumstances that have informed and motivated our
proposal for the installation of the ADA-compliant ramp:
1. On March 16, 2025, we had a memorial service scheduled following the sudden death of
one of our church and community leaders. A great windstorm arose, whipping us about at
the graveside and following us back to the church. The service was packed, with about 250
in attendance,including several people in wheelchairs and mobility scooters. During the
service, several times the lights browned out as the wind blew. I wondered to myself
(Kirianne Weaver), sitting in the front of the congregation: "How in the world would we get
everyone out of the sanctuary if the power were to go out?" Even if we could have carried
some of the people down the steps in their wheelchairs, there is no way we could have
safely managed to help a larger individual in a bariatric scooter who was present.
2. In the weeks following that memorial service, a professor at Ithaca College who had been
coming to our church came one Sunday with a new baby that she was fostering through the
NYS courts. She came to the front of the church as she always did, but realized she couldn't
get the stroller up the steps. A foster baby cannot be left in anyone's care but the foster
parent, so she had to hold the baby while making three trips up and down the steps: for her
bag, for the diaper bag, and for the stroller. Someone helped,but after three trips,she was
still exhausted, and we have never seen her in worship since.
3. Following the worship service on September 14, 2025, one of our spry older members
tripped while carrying something, fell, and broke his hip at the top of the steps next to the
lift. The ambulance was called,and they brought their gurney into the building. The EMTs
asked if we had any way they could roll up to the sanctuary level so they could wheel him
back to the ambulance: no, we didn't, though we mentioned that we have certainly been
trying. The EMTs had to roll him onto a blanket and carry him downstairs,slung between
6

them as in a hammock, while he gritted his teeth in pain. Imagine having a broken hip and
being swung down stairs in a sling.
How do the Design and Material Resonate with the Context?
The proposed design balances functional necessity with historic sensitivity. The material palette,
featuring concrete or precast elements in tones compatible with the buff Ohio sandstone and Indiana
limestone of the existing facade, ensures visual cohesionto the extent that it iseconomically and
physically possible. We are committedto salvaging and reusingexisting materials of the stairs that
will be replaced.
The rounded ramp option draws direct inspiration from the church’s Romanesque Revival feature,
specifically, its semi-circular arches. The soft curvature of the ramp complements these architectural
forms, echoing the building’s historic geometry without mimicking it. The rectilinear componentsalign
with the orthogonal logic of the building mass in context.

Principle #2 The historic features of a property located within, and

the historic character of the individual property and the character of the

physical, or pictorial evidence.
Standard #9 New additions, exterior alterations, or related new

property. The new work shall be differentiated from the old and shall be

As identified in the City of Ithaca’s Cornell Heights Historic District Summary
Statement, the period of significance for the area now known as the Cornell
Heights Historic District is 1898-1937.
As indicated in the New York State Building-Structure Inventory Form, the
Craftsman Style residence at 102 Triphammer Road was constructed between
1916 and 1917 for a Cornell University Professor of soil technology.
Constructed within the period of significance of the Cornell Heights Historic
District and possessing a high level of integrity, the property is a contributing
element of the Cornell Heights Historic District.
In consideration of this and all approvals of proposals for alterations, new
construction, or demolition in historic districts, the ILPC must determine
that the proposed exterior work will not have a substantial adverse effect
on the aesthetic, historical, or architectural significance and value of either
the landmark or, if the improvement is within a district, of the
neighboring improvements in such district. In considering architectural
and cultural value, the Commission shall consider whether the proposed
change is consistent with the historic value and the spirit of the
architectural style of the landmark or district in accordance with Section
228-6 of the Municipal Code. In making this determination, the
Commission is guided by the principles set forth in Section 228-6B of the
Municipal Code, as further elaborated in Section 228-6C, and by the
Secretary of the Interior’s Standards for Rehabilitation,and in this case
specifically the followingprinciples andStandards:
Principle #2 The historic features of a property located within, and
contributing to the significance of, an historic district shall be altered as
little as possible and any alterations made shall be compatible with both
the historic character of the individual property and the character of the
district as a whole.
Standard #2 The historic character of a property will be retained and
preserved. The removal of distinctive materials or alteration of features
and spaces that characterize a property will be avoided.
Standard #6 Deteriorated historic features shall be repaired rather than
replaced. When the severity of deterioration requires replacement of a
distinctive feature, the new feature shall match the old in design, color,
texture, and other visual qualities, and where possible, materials.
Replacement of missing features shall be substantiated by documentary,
physical, or pictorial evidence.
Standard #9 New additions, exterior alterations, or related new
construction shall not destroy historic materials that characterize the
property. The new work shall be differentiated from the old and shall be
