Crawford Door Company
Milestones
1930 Crawford Door is founded by Fred Crawford and three
or four other investors from Thompson Products of
Cleveland. The company begins to make garage doors
in “a back alley operation” in Detroit, Michigan.
1934 The Model SC sectional residential door with torsion
spring becomes the standard item. Prior to the SC, the
company made one-piece doors and some bi-fold doors.
Crawford is recognized as the first to develop the torsion
spring door.
1937 The company moves into a 12,000-square-foot plant at
5300 St. Jean Street in Detroit.
1940 Seeking better shipping facilities, the company moves to
a plant at 401 St. Jean.
1942 Production is converted to making aircraft parts during
the war, ca. 1942-45.
1947 Crawford holds their first national sales meeting with 52
distributors in attendance in Detroit.
1947 The Magician is first used as the company trademark,
and the name of “Marvel-Lift” doors is coined.
1949 Crawford’s first Distributor Advisory Board is appointed
to advise the company’s president, Bob Hackathorn.
1950 Crawford acquires a plant at 20263 Hoover Road, but
doesn’t make doors there until 1953.
1954 A plant is added at Phillipsburg, New Jersey, to handle
eastern hardware shipments.
1950sCrawford is widely recognized as the second largest
garage door manufacturer behind Overhead Door.
1955 The company has 95 distributors in the U.S. and Canada.
1959 Fred Crawford retires after creating TRW in 1958, a
merger of Thompson Products and Ramo &
Wooldridge. TRW became one of the nation’s premier
defense companies.
1960? Crawford builds a plant in Gottenborg, Sweden, and
eventually builds a large international presence. Today,
Crawford Door is a company in the Swedish Cardo
Door Group, operating in more than 30 countries in
Europe and Asia.
1962 Fred Crawford sells the domestic operations of Crawford
Door to Jim Walter Company.
1964 Jim Walter Company moves production from Detroit to
Ecorse, Michigan.
1974 Crawford headquarters moves from Detroit to Tampa.
1978 Crawford door manufacturing in Michigan ends.
1983 Wayne-Dalton purchases Crawford Door Co. from Jim
Walter Corp.
1994 Fred Crawford dies at the age of 103.

DESIGN REVIEW

As set forth in §73-4 of the Municipal Code, the Ithaca Landmarks Preservation Commission is
responsible for the review of proposed changes to the exteriors of locally designated properties. In
2025, the ILPC held nine regular and two special meetings and reviewed and approved 16 Certificate
of Appropriateness applications.
Under §228-6(c) the Commission may delegate to the Commission’s Secretary authority to approve certain
types of alterations, including ordinary maintenance and repairs, restorations, in-kind replacements and
work that is not highly visible from the public way. During the reporting period, the Commission’s Secretary
reviewed 168 permit applications and approved 99; the remaining 69 did not require approval as the
proposed work did not include exterior alterations.
REAL PROPERTY TAX EXEMPTIONS FOR HISTORIC PROPERTIES OR THE “ITHACA LAW” (444A-2)
In 1997, the City of Ithaca became the first New York State community to adopt a tax abatement program for
historic properties. In brief, the program provides a ten-year window of tax relief (City and County only) on
the increase in assessed value resulting from the substantial rehabilitation of or investment in a structure.
Property tax exemptions have been granted to 14 properties in the City’s locally designated historic districts
since 2000. In 2025, there were five properties enrolled in this property tax exemption and the total
property tax savings for the owners of these properties was $8,039. Viewed another way, this amount
represents the public’s investment in the maintenance and rehabilitation of the City’s designated historic
resources. Major projects that have utilized this incentive program include the Argos Inn at 408-10 E. State
St. in the East Hill Historic District, The William Henry Miller Inn at 303-07 N. Aurora St. in the East Hill
Historic District, and the former Red Cross residence at 201 S. Clinton St. in the Henry St. John Historic
District.
NEW YORK STATE HISTORIC HOMEOWNERSHIP REHABILITATION TAX CREDIT
This program provides an income tax credit equal to 20% of qualified rehabilitation expenditures, up to a
credit value of $50,000, for owner-occupied properties listed in the State and National Registers of Historic
Places. Applying for the credits involves completing a three-part application: Part I certifies the property as
being listed in the National Register of Historic Place and eligible for the program; Part II outlines the scope
and value of the proposed rehabilitation work; and Part III certifies the completion of the work as proposed
and the final cost. Properties within the City’s DeWitt Park, East Hill, Cornell Heights, and Henry St. John
Historic Districts qualify of this program. In 2025, eleven (11) property owners completed Parts I and II of
tax credit application; the total value of the proposed work was $482,601. Four (4) property owners
completed Part III of the application and were collectively awarded $37,974 in income tax credits. The
remaining 7 property owners can complete the proposed work and submit Part III of the application this
year.
HISTORIC RESOURCE IDENTIFICATION
The Planning Division once again partnered with Cornell University’s graduate program in historic
preservation planning to conduct a historic resource survey in 2025. The survey focused on the Belle
Sherman/Bryant Park neighborhood and is expected to continue for several years.
CERTIFIED LOCAL GOVERNMENT (CLG) ACTIVITIES
The primary goal of the Certified Local Government (CLG) program is to encourage municipalities to
develop and maintain community preservation efforts in coordination with local land use planning and
improvement activities. Participation in the CLG program allows municipalities to partner with the state
and federal governments throughout the processes of identifying and evaluating community resources
and protecting historic properties. The City of Ithaca became a Certified Local Government (CLG) on
October 10, 1986, and is one of the oldest CLG communities in New York State. As a CLG community, the

City is eligible to apply to the State Historic Preservation Office (SHPO) for funding to conduct
preservation-related activities. In the summer of 2024, the Commission Secretary applied for and
received two CLG Sub-grants for a historic resource survey of the DeWitt Park neighborhood and
preliminary work needed for a statewide LBGTQ historic context statement. The awards totaled
$54,000. Work on these projects started in early 2025 and will conclude in the second quarter of this
year.
